## Path and specialty

> I come from South Africa. I've been building and flying drones for more than ten years [site bio: more than / around 10 years; ~2016/2017]. I maintain my commercial pilot's licence in South Africa since 2019, referred to as RPC.

> In the Netherlands since 2021: specific category licence and SORA II (2) vergunning to operate around vital infrastructure and controlled airspace.

> I have been a vegan for many years and I have been fortunate enough to find people who showed me that we could do more for animals than just keep them off our plate. In the Netherlands I found people who developed skills to actually do more for animals. There are communities who dedicate themselves to developing skills and knowledge to do specific, high-impact things that save animals that I thought was impossible to do before.

> I volunteer with different organisations to do search and rescue with my thermal drone. My specialty is searching for animals.

## How searching started

> I started searching for animals when I went to an expo on Vliegveld Twente and met a fellow South African working for Robor, who introduced me to searching for animals and nests. I bought a thermal drone and attached myself to organisations who search for animals. I learned a lot from them.

## Method ceiling → repertoire (not a trade-off)

> The method that we used limited us to searching smaller areas. It is still valid in a small area when we are searching for types of nests from birds that tend to cover the nests. A search operation always needs to maximise resources.

> If I needed to search larger areas or only needed to send someone out into the field instead of making them go out to follow the drone, I needed to be able to add other methods of search to my repertoire and make that available to others too.

> Because I had my own kit, I could develop those methods. (Own kit = enabler. Do not put "with my own kit" in how the old method is described. Do not say "for years" about that method. Do not call it a "trade-off.")

## Build and AI

> I've been designing, building and maintaining secure IT infrastructure since 1997. I could not build an app. I use AI to build out features for the OpenSAR platform, which includes an app.

> People might think that an app is just something that sits on your phone, however there is quite a lot of infrastructure that I maintain to make it all work.

> My professional background means I can make decisions on what technologies to use, how to make it fit together, how to secure it and how to keep it running, which is beyond what an AI agent may be able to do at the moment.

## Pricing

> I price the app based on how much I expect the user to tax the infrastructure, so development time isn't something built into the cost. Truthfully, token cost isn't much compared to paying developers.

> I built what I needed to conduct my searches and that's sunk cost already. I commercialised OpenSAR so that others can use the platform and they pay for whatever it costs to keep it running, beyond what I need it to run.

> It takes this to keep OpenSAR running, and you only pay for your load on it. (Dashboard visual; no VMs / networking / stack tour unless he asks.)

## Mission (on camera; not the terms laundry list)

> OpenSAR is designed to support activities that protect and preserve life. At the heart of it, we search for people and animals to save their lives.

> We need complete situational awareness of everyone involved in a search and things that can affect an operation. OpenSAR is a single platform that does that for us.

> We don't need expensive equipment or multiple systems that don't talk to each other. We need a phone and a drone; that's it.
