## The test

Every sentence under his name must pass: *would Clinton say this out loud in an interview, or to a colleague across a table?* If not, rewrite it until he would. Nothing else in this skill overrides that.

## How he writes

0. **No em dashes. Ever.** Do not use `—` (U+2014) in anything written in his voice: CV bullets, letters, LinkedIn, bios, proposals, skill examples that model his voice. Use a comma, full stop, colon, or parentheses. (Clinton, 2026-10-17.)
1. **The approach follows from what you start with.** He does not declare one way is right. He says which conditions call for which approach, and what each is limited by. On the guides: "The search method you employ comes from what information you start with." In a CV or letter: context (company size, who he reported to, the constraint) before the claim.
2. **Plain statement, then "because" / "this means" / "so".** A claim is followed by its reason in the same breath. "This is the most time consuming method because it means the drone cannot continue searching…" / "I built reusable AI agent skills that capture how we investigate incidents, so the next person does not start from scratch."
3. **Numbers are hedged and conditional.** "about 8ha", "about 1500 employees", "approximately 150 stores", "more than ten years", "roughly". Never a round triumph number without its condition. Prefer "more than 10 years" over nailing 2016 vs 2017 when the bio hedges. Never invent a number. Facts live in `resume-writer/references/facts.md` or `clinton-cv`.
4. **Cost and place, not triumph, and not "trade-off" as a label.** State what something is limited by and when it still works. He rejected framing an old method as a "trade-off." Say: it limited us to smaller areas; it is still valid when…; when I needed X, I needed other approaches. Maximise resources. In career writing: what he did and what constrained it, not "drove excellence."
5. **Everyday analogy once, then back to the work.** The airliner window for height and speed. One per piece, if any. Never a metaphor stack.
6. **Pronouns.** "I" freely for path, practice, and CV ("I come from South Africa", "I was responsible for…", "I volunteer with…"). "We" for the team on the job ("With my fellow team members I was responsible for…", "the method we used"). "You" when instructing a reader. Never "we" meaning a company brand voice.
7. **Admits what is unfinished or what AI cannot do, flat, same breath.** "TBC." "beyond what an AI agent may be able to do at the moment." A limit sits next to the claim, not softened and not saved for a confession beat. Scope limits (Jamf, ServiceNow) go in the letter, phrased positively, not as CV disclaimers.
8. **No hype.** No exclamation marks, no "game-changer", no "simply", no "powerful", no "passionate", no "tell me what breaks", no PoC language for working work. Strongest words: "reliable", "acceptable", "useful", "responsible for", "managed", "built", "truthfully".
9. **Named specifics, not atmosphere.** Employers, products, places, ticket names, years: Allan Gray, Meraki, Just Eat Takeaway, Vliegveld Twente, Robor, RPC, SORA II vergunning, 1997, 2019, 2021. Keep real names (including Dutch *vergunning*). Do not invent drama around them.
10. **Enabler in the turn, not in the room.** "I had my own kit" / AI / career background is why he *could* do the next thing, not how the old situation is dressed up. Do not say "for years I…" when he only did something, then changed. Duration inflation is false.
11. **Misconception, then correction.** "People might think an app is just something on your phone, there is quite a lot of infrastructure…". Gentle, not a dunk. Useful for LinkedIn and bios; rare in CV bullets.
12. **Price / cost is load and running cost when the topic is OpenSAR.** Tax the infrastructure; development is sunk; others pay to keep it running beyond what he needs. Not value-based pricing theatre. Irrelevant to most CV bullets; relevant to OpenSAR LinkedIn or proposals.
13. **Specialty as practice, not a badge.** "I volunteer with different organisations to do search and rescue with my thermal drone. My specialty is searching for animals." Two plain sentences. Same shape for career: say the practice, not the title inflation.
14. **Understated path.** "Fortunate enough to find people who showed me…" / "things I thought were impossible before." No hero arc. One thing moves into the next (golden thread); do not reorder his chapters for punch. CV chronology follows facts.md, not a punchy reorder.
15. **OpenSAR product CTA is kit, not trial-as-PoC.** "Your drone, a phone and OpenSAR. You don't need anything else." Not "try it and tell me what breaks." For career CTAs in letters: "I would welcome the chance to talk about the role."

Describe the voice in **habits with quotes**, never in adjectives ("friendly", "authoritative").

## His words

### Career and professional prose

| He says | Not |
|---|---|
| I was responsible for, I managed, I built, I project managed, I advised on, I worked with, I implemented, I reported to | owned, leveraged, spearheaded, drove, codified, delivered excellence |
| 3rd party vendors, 3rd line support, day to day, business cases, stakeholders | supplier governance, cross-functional initiatives, operational excellence |
| about / approximately \<n\>, more than \<n\>, \<n\>+ when the floor is real | inflated round numbers, "countless", "numerous" |
| the fix goes into the standard, not just the ticket; so the next person does not start from scratch | structural fix, institutionalised learning, knowledge management framework |
| working knowledge of… (in a letter) | not a specialist in… (in the CV body) |
| comma, full stop, colon, parentheses | em dash (`—`) |

|---|---|

## Turning a slogan into his sentence

| Slogan (not him) | Clinton |
|---|---|
| Spearheaded multi-site rollout across the estate. | I project managed network hardware deployments for approximately 150 stores worldwide. |
| Owned network architecture; translated risk policy into security controls. | I was responsible for the networking infrastructure at the largest private asset management company in South Africa: 7 offices and about 1500 employees. I implemented security systems based on policy from risk and compliance. |
| Built reusable AI skills that encode runbooks so issues are fixed structurally. | I built reusable AI agent skills that capture how we investigate incidents and review changes, so the next person does not start from scratch. |
| Results-driven architect with a proven track record in retail. | (cut: say what he did at which retailer, with a number from facts.md) |
| Nobody should have to wait for anybody. | The ground team and the drone search are decoupled. The pilot marks and moves on; the ground team evaluates the subject and goes to it at their convenience. |
| I stopped hovering over finds. | Hovering is fine on a small field with covered nests, up to about 8 ha. Past that I mark and move on, because the window is about 90 minutes. |
| The method was a trade-off. | The method we used limited us to smaller areas. It is still valid on a small field when the birds cover their nests. When I needed larger areas, or only needed to send someone into the field instead of making them follow the drone, I needed other methods in the repertoire. |
| For years I struggled with bad tools. | I searched that way, then needed other methods. (Do not invent "for years.") |
| I had my own kit so I searched differently. | Because I had my own kit, I could develop those methods and make them available to others. (Kit = enabler, not the old method.) |
| AI makes OpenSAR cheap and magical. | I use AI to build out features. Token cost is not much compared to paying developers. My background is deciding technologies, how they fit, security, and keeping it running, beyond what an AI agent may be able to do at the moment. |
| Try it and tell me what breaks. | Your drone, a phone and OpenSAR. You don't need anything else. |
| I can do animals and people. | I volunteer with different organisations to do search and rescue with my thermal drone. My specialty is searching for animals. |

Left column = résumé-speak or marketing slogan. Right column = something he would say. When he corrects a line, add the pair here.

## When to apply

- CV bullets, profile, "In short" lines, cover letters
- LinkedIn About, posts, comments under his name
- Bios, proposals, bid text, emails where he is the author
- OpenSAR origin, pricing, and guide-style explanations in prose (not Rover's spoken lines; those stay in the videos repo)

Not a substitute for facts: this skill is how he writes, not what he has done. Facts stay in `resume-writer/references/facts.md` / `clinton-cv`.

## Check before handing a line back

- Does it say *when* this applies and *what limits it*, or does it declare?
- Is the number hedged ("about", "approximately", "up to", "depending on", "more than"), and is it in facts.md?
- Is the reason in the sentence ("because", "so", "this means")?
- Would it sit next to a paragraph from the Methods guide, his P21 flesh, or his source CV without a change of tone?
- Does it leave the other approach its place, without calling it a "trade-off"?
- Did we put the enabler (own kit, AI, career) in the turn, not in the room?
- Did we invent duration ("for years"), struggle, or PoC language he did not use?
- For origin or LinkedIn path lines: is this close to how he fleshed it, or did we "improve" it into a slogan?
- Are there any em dashes (`—`)? If yes, remove them.
- Would he say it out loud in an interview?

## "That doesn't sound like me"

Ask him to say the point out loud in his own words. Write that down verbatim. Add the pair (draft → his line) to the slogan table above and to `resume-writer/references/voice.md` Rewrite examples. If a pattern produced the miss, fix the pattern.
