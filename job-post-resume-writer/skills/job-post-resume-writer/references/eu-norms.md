# EU application norms (this version's default locale)

Applies to applications in EU and EEA countries plus the UK and Switzerland unless the posting says otherwise. Recorded in `config.json` as `"locale": "eu"`.

## Layout

- A4, two pages for anyone with more than five years' experience; one page for graduates.
- No photo by default. Exceptions where a photo is still common: Germany, Austria, Switzerland, parts of Southern and Eastern Europe. Even there it is optional; leave it out unless the person wants it, and never embed one in the screening copy.
- Dates as `Mon yyyy – Mon yyyy` (e.g. `Mar 2021 – Present`); write the month, do not rely on dd/mm order.
- Spelling: follow the language of the posting. British English for English postings from EU employers unless the company is clearly US-styled.
- Phone in international format (`+31 6 …`).

## Content

- Do **not** include: date of birth, age, marital status, nationality (unless it answers a right-to-work question), religion, photo, full address (city and country are enough), references ("available on request" is noise).
- **Right to work**: state it in one line when it is not obvious from the location, using the local wording. Netherlands example: "Residence permit — arbeid vrij toegestaan, TWV niet vereist". EU citizens applying within the EU: "EU citizen" is enough. Non-EU nationals: name the permit type; employers screen for sponsorship.
- **Languages**: one line with CEFR-style levels or plain words (native / fluent / conversational). Many EU postings list the local language as an advantage; say it honestly.
- **Location and office pattern**: city plus "near <major city>" when it matters; if the posting states days on site, the letter can say commuting is practical.
- **Availability / notice period**: common to state in the letter for Dutch, German and Belgian employers.
- GDPR: no consent clause is needed on the CV itself; some Spanish and Polish employers still request one in the application form, not the CV.

## Titles and degrees

- Keep the original degree name (BSc, MSc, BCom (Hons), Diplom, Laurea) and add a short gloss only if the posting's market will not recognise it ("honours degree, one year postgraduate").
- Local certification names stay as issued (e.g. ITIL 4 Foundation, PL-300).

## What the installer records

`locale: "eu"` only. Country-specific choices (photo, language of the CV) are decided per application in `match.md`.
